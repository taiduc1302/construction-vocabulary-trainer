# Connecting an existing CB lead to a new storm main — vocabulary

Six independent entries were added to the **Underground Utilities** learning pack so learners can distinguish a branch connection, a pipe-end joint and a change in diameter/material. The existing `catch-basin-lead` term was also included in the pack and given searchable aliases such as **CB lead**.

| The term on the drawing / in a conversation | What the estimator should understand |
| --- | --- |
| **Saddle connection** | A saddle assembly attached around a prepared opening in the existing main, *only when allowed by design and authority* |
| **Wye fitting** | A manufactured angled branch integrated into a main run; for an existing main, the work may involve replacing a cut-out segment |
| **Pipe coupling** | A fitting that joins two pipe ends; it does **not** create a new lateral outlet in the main |
| **Short pipe section** | A cut-to-length piece to reach a connection point; additional couplings, bends and joint allowances may be required |
| **Pipe reducer** | A fitting to change nominal pipe size; consider eccentric/concentric geometry and gravity sewer invert |
| **Transition coupling** | A compatible coupling for different pipe materials, outside diameters and/or joint types, subject to specification |

## Estimate/takeoff reminder

For the actual tie-in, check the **approved connection detail**, both pipe materials and outside diameters, proposed and existing **invert elevations**, size, gradient, location of junction, necessary excavation and replacement length, couplings, bends, bedding, access and testing. A mismatch in elevations cannot be assumed correctable with just a coupling and one short straight section.

**Do not automatically select saddle instead of wye.** Requirements vary between municipalities and across main size/material. The diagrams are generic learning illustrations, **not engineered details or authorizations to cut into a sewer main**.

## Public reference material used for terminology

- City of Vancouver, [Standard Detail Drawings, Sewer Connection Standards S7.1](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/standard-detail-drawings-storm-and-sanitary-sewers.pdf): manufactured wyes/saddles, replacing a short main section for a new wye, approved adaptors, bend limitations.
- City of Coquitlam, [Supplementary Specifications to MMCD, Storm Sewers §33 40 01S (2022)](https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/342/Supplementary-Specifications-and-Detailed-Drawings-to-MMCD-PDF): municipality-specific distinctions between a manufactured wye and an insertable tee on existing mains.
- [Fernco Canada, Flexible Tap Saddles](https://www.fernco.ca/products/flexible-tap-saddles): a manufacturer's example of a branch saddle and available tee/wye configurations.
- [Fernco, Standard Flexible Couplings](https://www.fernco.com/products/above-ground-connections/standard-couplings): coupling compatibility by material and outside diameter, with buried-use restrictions.
- [Fernco, Eccentric Couplings](https://www.fernco.com/products/eccentric-bushings-couplings): why gravity flowline alignment can matter when pipe sizes change.

Reference links are **background teaching material**, not claims that any specific product is approved for a project. Always follow the current issued contract drawings, owner specifications and approved manufacturer details.
